1. At a high level, I had to take a couple of different approaches to this project before I found one that worked to my satisfaction. At first, I tried the agentic build, wrote a thorough SPEC, let cursor run and started debugging once the agentic process concluded. And then I realized that the approach it had chosen to take was fundamentally flawed and was not even the right type of program I needed to get my intended result. Then, I went and did thorough research on my own about different parts of the OS that I could modify and I found something that was closer. I tried the agentic workflow again with this new process, and it still wasn’t making anything good. Then I took a detour (and deep dive) into the different moving parts that controlled the display and how the desktop receives input. I formed an idea for how to modify them to get my intended result and mandated those changes from the AI, one at a time. When there would be a new and uncertain feature, I would always discuss with it about different ways to implement the feature and then with that and my own research, I would direct the AI.

2. I coded entirely in cursor because I needed something that could read a lot of code. Within cursor, I did most tasks with the auto model, but for some more intensive tasks or things that model was bad at, I would switch to using Claude Opus 4.7. I kind of used an agentic process like I described above, but I broke it down into smaller steps, had the AI help plan and provided strong guidance about the approach to take.

3. I started with the agentic model because it had worked really well for the agentic homework as well as my phone app. However, when it failed spectacularly I recognized that a SPEC.md wouldn’t give me enough input at the right times. So, I looked back on the workflows I used for different parts of the course and I saw that the approach of having it build features with strong guidance worked really well for me in crossy road, so I thought I’d try it here.

4. I certainly wouldn’t have used cursor, or even thought that I could take on something of this size. I think that even if I had determined to do something like this and that I’d use AI, I wouldn’t have approached it in the right way, like having it do the whole thing while I just complain about bugs and ask it to fix them.

5. There are a couple of things I could do with more time, and if I had plenty of time I’d do all of them. Right now I got cut off working on scrolling integration with apps (I have one IDE mostly working). But, I’d also want things like a pdf viewer, maybe a slideshow presenter, or anything else that might be helpful. I also had to make a couple of decisions that made the final product be not quite as polished by working a little more in the shell extension level rather than the window manager and compositor level. For one, the way that the strokes are following windows around now is by just receiving information about where those windows are from the window manager and drawing everything that should be drawn in a single layer on top of everything else. It works for an MVP, but I’d really love to attach an annotation surface to every window. Making them part of the windows would prevent the annotations from lagging behind a bit when moving windows, and also make the annotations still be visible when the windows aren’t in their usual shape (like overview).
